TwoFold

AI-powered food waste reduction app

Personal project·2025Live

A kitchen app that knows what's in your pantry, warns you before it expires, and turns what's about to go bad into dinner.

Try the live demoView the codeExplore as a guest, no account needed
  • 50%less food wasted in our household

Situation

I don't like wasting food. Groceries get pushed to the back of the fridge, forgotten, and found after they've gone bad. My wife was constantly telling her AI assistant what groceries we had so it could suggest ideas for dinner.

Task

Build one place to manage our household's kitchen: an app that already knows what we have, even the things we forgot we bought, and helps us use it before it goes bad.

Action

I built TwoFold, a web app that installs like a phone app and works offline.

  • Snap to stock. Take a photo of your grocery haul or your receipt, and AI picks out every item. Receipts took a lot of fine-tuning to get right.
  • Expiry prediction. When a date isn't visible, TwoFold predicts when each item will expire, so you can cook whatever is going bad first.
  • Hands-off inventory. Make a recipe and its ingredients come out of your pantry automatically.
  • Clean recipes. Paste a recipe link or video, and TwoFold pulls the recipe out from under the ads, reformats it into easy steps, and builds the grocery list.
  • Waste tracking. It estimates what the groceries you throw out cost, so you can see how much money is going to waste.
  • Group events. Describe a potluck or group dinner and it sets up the event, sends invites, and tracks RSVPs and who's bringing what.
  • Reliable AI. Each AI feature runs in its own server function with its own key and retries on its own when Gemini is busy.

Result

TwoFold cut our household's food waste in half. It also taught me the most about what not to do.

What it taught me: Having everything isn't always the best solution. I kept adding features until the app was hard to manage from a user's point of view. When friends tested it, they thought it was an interesting idea, but didn't know what to do with it because there was so much going on.
